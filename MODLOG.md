## 2026-10-17 Test Track pale road: cause found, not fixed

**Changed:** nothing in code. Test textures were painted solid colours in the installed data and restored from a backup (`data/work/tt_tex_backup`).
**Why:** Xenia's Test Track road is mid-dark grey asphalt; ours is pale beige.
**Tested how:** painted every texture a solid colour and shot the start line; painted a handful of candidates (43, 45, 46, 51, 53) separately; read the pixel shader of model 16 (`fm2_show`).
**Result:** the road is not drawn from a colour texture. Painting texture 53 green turned the whole road green: 53 is the draw's baked lightmap tile, so the road is "baked road colour x a bright lightmap". The road draws (models 16, 43, 516, 546...) list only BC4 one-channel textures and carry pixel-shader constants `c1` = 1.2, `c2` = 0.1, `c3` = 0.1, `c4` = 0.3, `c5` = 2.0 (other road draws: `c1` = -0.05, `c3` = 0.6, `c5` = 2.5). The shader fetches three of those textures with `.xyz` and blends them with the constants. The importer treats `c1` as the road's colour, so 1.2 gives near-white.
**Still broken / not tested:** nothing fixed. A correct fix means reading how the three layers and `c0..c5` combine in that shader (the Suzuka `c3` gain was a partial version of the same idea). Texture 46 turned out to be the yellow line, not the road.
**Next:** decode the 3-layer road shader (or add a per-map road colour as a stopgap).

### Follow-up: first decode attempt (nothing changed in code)

Read the full pixel shader of model 16, group 2 (the road family with `c1` = 1.2). It fetches `tf4`, `tf5`, `tf6` (colour, `.xyz`), `tf3` (a mask) and `tf2`, then builds three palette terms from one sample `v = c0 - tf6.rgb`: `v*c5`, `v*c4 + c3`, `v*c2`, and `v*c1 - v*c2`; the `tf5` luma (weights 0.114 / 0.299 / 0.587) picks between them, and the result is multiplied by the sun and ambient terms. So `c1..c5` are palette gains and offsets, NOT a road colour: the importer's `c1`-as-colour reading is wrong for this family. Not resolved: `c0` is also multiplied into the sun term (line 64) and is 0 on some draws and 1 on others, so what each constant means per draw depends on the group; the swizzles in the dump (`r11.zxyy` and similar) make hand-reading error-prone. Two ways forward: write a small interpreter that runs the shader's microcode on the sampled textures (data-driven, large), or pick a per-map road colour from the Xenia shot (quick, guesswork).

## 2026-10-27 (76): the car's light block is traced: D3DX spherical-harmonic lights x per-vertex transfer data in the car model (finding + parser field)
**Where it comes from (PPC, `data/work/fm2_xex/text.asm`):** the render-item function at `0x8251f0c8` fills the item's light pointer (`item + 0xa0`, the "per-object block" of entry 52). It builds three sets of 16 floats (R, G, B; order-4 spherical harmonics) with two D3DX library calls: `0x82745f00` = `D3DXSHEvalHemisphereLight(4, up = (0,1,0), top, bottom, R, G, B)` and `0x82745dc8` = `D3DXSHEvalDirectionalLight(4, light direction, colour, R, G, B)`, and adds them. The inputs are the track's `<SHLighting>` (loader at `0x82579130`, struct order verified from the strings: SHTop R,G,B at +0, SHBottom at +0xc, SHDir at +0x18, SHEnvMapTop/Bottom at +0x24/+0x28). A second hemisphere call with SHEnvMapTop/Bottom builds the env block (VS `shEnv0..3`, c116..c119). The light direction is a vector in the render item (+0x80..0x88); the block is rebuilt only when it changes and shared by all cars. `0x82733ad0` (see entry 52) is `D3DXSHRotate` into the car's frame. This is why the executable contains no SH basis constants: they live in the D3DX library.
**What the vertex shader does with it:** it does not evaluate the SH at the normal. It dots four per-vertex vec4 inputs (texcoord1..4, 16 numbers) with the 16 SH coefficients of R, G, B and Env (`o6.xyz` = lit colour, `o6.w` = env visibility). That is precomputed radiance transfer (PRT): the self-shadowing of the body is baked into the model.
**Where the 16 numbers are in the file:** the 60-byte carbin vertex has them in its undecoded tail, bytes 28..60 of the vertex = 16 big-endian signed 16-bit values (VERIFIED on the Skyline GT-R V-spec II, 66k vertices): the first is about 16400 (0.50) on open surfaces; the next three follow -y, +z, -x of the surface direction (up: -15800 on the y one; +z side: +17000 on the z one; +x: -17100 on the x one), i.e. the D3DX real SH order l=0, l=1 (y, z, x with D3DX signs), then l=2, l=3. The first 12 tail bytes hold the normal / tangent frame in an encoding not decoded (the first short follows the z of the normal, 0.98 correlation); not needed, normals come from the triangles.
**Changed:** `fh1-formats/src/carbin_fm2.rs`: `Vertex::tail` (bytes 16..60, undecoded, no behaviour change); new example `carbin_fm2_tail` (writes a CSV of vertex, normal, tail bytes for analysis, to a path you give it).
**Not done yet:** using the transfer data in the engine's car paint.
